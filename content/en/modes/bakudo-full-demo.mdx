## Combat Fundamentals: Reading the Ball, Not the Health Bar

BAKUDO's hook is that it treats a ball sport like a fighting game. Combat is real-time, fast, and built around dynamic ball-throwing techniques rather than sword swings or gunfire. The loop is observation first, execution second: watch how a boss moves, learn the pattern, then exploit the gap.

| Situation | What to Do | Mistake to Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Boss begins a wind-up | Watch the release point, not the character's face | Panic-dodging before the throw commits |
| You're stuck on defense | Reset spacing and wait for a readable tell | Trading hits to "get damage back" |
| A pattern repeats | Bank the timing and counter on the second pass | Assuming the third pass looks identical |
| You just took a big hit | Breathe, re-establish distance, re-read | Charging in while still tilted |

Community reports from the demo's review page consistently point to the same lesson: the players who improve fastest are the ones who treat each loss as a scouting run. The boss rush format rewards pattern recognition over raw reaction speed, and that is exactly why the BAKUDO full demo is a useful training ground — two bosses is enough to teach the language without overwhelming you.

### Practical Tips for the Two Boss Fights

- **Play the tutorial twice if you're new to the genre.** The second pass is where the timing actually clicks.
- **Use a controller.** The store page explicitly recommends it, and analog input helps with the micro-positioning that ball combat demands.
- **Learn one boss at a time.** Don't bounce between attempts; repetition builds the pattern memory the fights are designed around.
- **Watch the ball, not the boss.** The projectile is the tell.
- **Take breaks after three or four wipes.** Tilt is real, and the demo's difficulty tags suggest the developers expect it.
