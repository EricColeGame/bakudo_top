## BAKUDO Full Demo Walkthrough, Stage by Stage

### Stage 1 — The Combat Tutorial: Practice, Don't Pass

The tutorial is short, but it's the only place the demo explains itself. Treat it as a lab rather than a formality. Test how the ball travels, how quickly you recover after a swing, and how much space you actually need to land a clean hit. If a prompt tells you to try something, try it three or four times instead of once.

The most common mistake here is treating the tutorial as a corridor. It isn't. Players who spend an extra five minutes experimenting consistently report fewer wall-like deaths on the second boss.

### Stage 2 — The Tutorial Boss: Learn to Read, Not to Rush

The tutorial boss exists to teach you that this game is about reading the opponent. It telegraphs generously, and it gives you room to recover from a mistake. Use that. Your goal in this fight isn't a fast clear — it's building the habit of watching for a wind-up, holding your position, and only committing when the opening is real.

If you die here, that's information, not a verdict. Player experience suggests most first deaths come from attacking during recovery frames rather than from the boss doing anything unreadable.

### Stage 3 — The Early-Stage Boss: Where the Demo Opens Up

This is the fight the demo is actually about. The early-stage boss compresses everything the tutorial taught you: tighter timing, smaller windows, and far less forgiveness for panic inputs. Expect to lose several times. Expect the loss to feel fair once you see what killed you.

The reliable approach is a three-step loop: survive one full cycle of the boss's pattern without attacking, identify the single safest punish window, then build your damage around that one window. Once that's stable, add a second window. Players who try to learn the whole moveset at once tend to stall.

| Stage | Your goal | What to practice | Where players get stuck |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tutorial | Absorb the controls | Ball timing, recovery, spacing | Skipping prompts |
| Tutorial boss | Learn to read telegraphs | Patience, punishing only real openings | Attacking too early |
| Early-stage boss | Build a repeatable damage loop | One safe punish window at a time | Panic inputs after a hit |

## Making the Ball-Based Boss-Rush Click

BAKUDO's combat identity is the ball. It isn't decorative — it's the thing that shapes your range, your timing, and your risk. A BAKUDO full demo walkthrough that treats the ball as a generic weapon will feel vague, so here's how to think about it instead.

| Principle | Why it matters | How to apply it |
|---|---|---|
| Know your range before you commit | The ball creates distance you have to respect | Walk the arena in the tutorial and note where hits land |
| Treat every swing as a decision | Recovery is where deaths happen | Only attack when you've already identified the opening |
| Hold center ground | Corners remove your escape options | Circle rather than backpedal |
| Reset after a hit | Tilt produces repeated mistakes | Back off, breathe, re-enter on your terms |
| Run the same fight twice in a row | Pattern recognition compounds | Re-queue immediately after a clear |

Three drills worth running in the tutorial arena:

- **The no-attack run.** Survive a full boss cycle without attacking once. You'll learn more in 90 seconds than in five failed attempts.
- **The one-window run.** Land damage only in a single, specific opening. Everything else is movement.
- **The clean-exit run.** Practice disengaging after every hit rather than chaining attacks.
