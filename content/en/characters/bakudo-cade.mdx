## What Is Bakudō? The Binding Half of Kidō

If you searched for BAKUDO Cade, you almost certainly want Bakudō — the binding half of Bleach's kidō system and one of the most misunderstood power sets in the series. Bakudō wins fights without drawing blood: it pins limbs, seals movement, raises barriers, and even carries messages across a battlefield. This BAKUDO Cade guide breaks the entire system down, from the number-one restraint spell to the forbidden seals in the nineties.

Kidō, literally "demon arts," is the Shinigami spellcraft taught at the Shin'ō Academy and split into three families. Hadō destroys. Kaidō heals. Bakudō restrains, defends, and supports. A Soul Reaper with mediocre swordsmanship but elite Bakudō control can still end a fight in two seconds flat.

| Family | Japanese | Core Purpose | Signature Example | Typical Role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hadō | 破道 | Destruction | #33 Sōkatsui | Direct damage |
| Bakudō | 縛道 | Binding and defense | #61 Rikujōkōrō | Control, capture, protection |
| Kaidō | 回道 | Healing | Unnumbered healing arts | Recovery and support |

Bakudō is not a "weaker" branch of kidō. It is the branch that decides whether a fight happens on your terms. Capture spells end chases, barriers buy seconds, and communication spells coordinate entire squads. ## How the Bakudō Numbering System Works

Every Bakudō is named and numbered. The number signals difficulty, reiatsu cost, and casting time — not raw destructive output. A #1 spell is a party trick a first-year student can manage; a #90-plus spell can reshape a battlefield and is often restricted even inside the Gotei 13.

One reason BAKUDO Cade searches spike after major anime fight scenes is the numbering itself: viewers hear a number shouted and immediately want to know what it means. The table below maps the tiers.

| Tier | Numbers | Difficulty | What to Expect | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Entry | 1–20 | Low | Fast, cheap, single-target restraint | Sai, Hainawa, Seki, Geki, Hōrin |
| Utility | 21–40 | Moderate | Stealth, escape, barriers, rescue | Sekienton, Kyokkō, Shitotsu Sansen, Tsuriboshi, Enkōsen |
| Advanced | 41–60 | High | Tracking, communication, wide-area control | Kakushitsuijaku |
| Elite | 61–80 | Very high | Multi-limb binding, crushing force, area barriers | Rikujōkōrō, Hyapporankan, Sajō Sabaku, Tozanshō, Gochūtekkan, Tenteikūra, Kuyō Shibari |
| Forbidden | 81–99 | Extreme | Void-level barriers and total seals | Dankū, Kin, Bankin |

Worth noting: fan-made wikis and roleplay communities regularly invent their own Bakudō, sometimes numbering past 99. Community reports treat those as homebrew, not canon, so keep official and fanon spell lists separate when you are researching.

## Incantations, Abandonment, and Casting Speed

Every Bakudō has an incantation, and reciting it in full produces the spell at maximum strength. Skipping the words — called incantation abandonment, or *eishō-haki* — trades power for speed. That trade-off is the single most important thing to understand about Bakudō, and it comes up in nearly every BAKUDO Cade discussion.

A clean example is Bakudō #61, Rikujōkōrō, whose full chant runs: "Carriage of thunder, bridge of a spinning wheel. With light, divide this into six." Say it and the rods hit harder and hold longer. Drop it and the spell fires instantly, but a strong opponent may shatter it.

| Casting Style | Speed | Power | Reiatsu Cost | Best Scenario |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Full incantation | Slow | Maximum | High | Fighting above your weight class |
| Abandoned incantation | Instant | Reduced | Moderate | Interrupting a fast attacker |
| Partial or modified chant | Medium | Middle ground | Moderate | Skilled casters buying efficiency |

Skilled users also chain spells: bind the arms with Hainawa, then land a heavier seal while the target is still fighting the first. The real skill is sequencing, not memorizing numbers.

## Countering Bakudō: Barriers, Reiatsu, and Escape

A BAKUDO Cade search often ends with the same question: how do you get out? The series gives a few consistent answers, and they double as useful rules for fan discussions and tabletop adaptations.

- **Overwhelming reiatsu.** A vastly stronger fighter can simply burst a low-numbered binding apart.
- **Barrier spells.** Dankū (#81) and Enkōsen (#39) neutralize incoming attacks rather than the bindings themselves.
- **Speed before the cast completes.** Abandoned incantations are fast, but a full chant leaves an opening.
- **Physical leverage.** Some restraints only lock the arms; a skilled fighter can still kick, headbutt, or fall.
- **Outside help.** Tenteikūra and Kakushitsuijaku mean a bound ally is rarely truly alone.

| Counter Method | Works Best Against | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Reiatsu burst | Spells #1–40 | Fails against elite bindings |
| Dankū barrier | Hadō follow-ups | Does not remove existing bindings |
| Pre-emptive speed | Full-incantation casts | Useless against abandoned casts |
| Ally support | Any binding | Requires a nearby teammate |

The pattern is simple: Bakudō rewards preparation and punishes hesitation. If you know a caster is coming, close the distance before they finish the chant.

## FAQ

**Is BAKUDO Cade a real spell in Bleach?**
No. BAKUDO Cade is a search phrase, not an official technique. The canonical spells are numbered Bakudō such as Sai (#1), Rikujōkōrō (#61), and Dankū (#81).

**What is the strongest Bakudō in the series?**
Bakudō #99, Kin and Bankin, is the highest-numbered binding shown, followed closely by #81 Dankū, which is described as capable of stopping Hadō up to the high 80s.

**Can Bakudō be cast without the incantation?**
Yes. Incantation abandonment (*eishō-haki*) lets a caster fire instantly at reduced power, which is why skilled fighters often skip the chant entirely.

**Who is the best Bakudō user in Bleach?**
Tessai Tsukabishi, the former Grand Kidō Chief of the Kidō Corps, is the standard answer, with Hachigen Ushōda and Byakuya Kuchiki close behind in on-screen demonstrations of high-level binding work.
