## Reading the Reveal: A Two-Pass Viewing Method

Most people watch a trailer once, absorb the vibe, and move on. That is fine for entertainment, but it is a poor way to extract information. A two-pass approach gets you much more out of a short reveal.

**First pass — feel it.** Watch start to finish without pausing. Note the tone, the camera language, the music genre, and how the title card lands. Ask yourself what emotion the cut is selling: wonder, tension, mischief, nostalgia?

**Second pass — read it.** Pause on every frame that lingers. Look for signage, uniforms, interface elements, and any text baked into the environment. These are the details that survive into the final game and usually hint at systems the marketing team is not ready to explain yet.

| Pass | Focus | What to note |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tone and art direction | Color palette, camera style, music genre |
| 1 | Title card | Exact wording, platform logos, any copyright line |
| 2 | Environment | Signage, uniforms, props, architecture |
| 2 | Interface | Menus, HUD elements, icons |
| 2 | On-screen text | Dates, ratings, studio credits |
| 2 | Technical read | Perceived frame rate and image clarity |

The word doing the heaviest lifting in this reveal is "Academy." An academy setting implies structure: a roster of students, a progression ladder, a hub location, maybe a curriculum that doubles as a tutorial. That is inference, not confirmation — but it is the kind of inference that tends to hold up, because studios rarely put "Academy" in a title unless the school is load-bearing.
